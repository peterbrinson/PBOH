Choices
<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>
Risk and Reward
When a game presents two (or more) paths of unequal challenge and outcome.
In the case of the Mario 64 level pictured here, these might be literal paths. Both lead to the same place, but one of them is more dangerous and filled with treasures.

Choices

Super Mario 64 (1996)

![[slide_13_img_281.png]]
<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>
Builds
Something you choose at the beginning of the game that affects your experience throughout.
In Mass Effect, you choose a class at the start of the game that affects your abilities and weapon use for the rest of the game. In Hades, you choose a weapon that determines your attacks for that run. In, for example, Magic: The Gathering, you build a deck that determines what cards you have access to and the probability of drawing them during the game.

Choices

Hades (2020)

![[slide_14_img_296.jpg]]
<br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br><br>
Progression
Sometimes the game can present you with choices as you play that affect your strategy and allow you to determine your play style. In Baldur’s Gate 3, you can choose your class and class options every time you level up. In Hades, you can choose whether you want to seek boons from a particular god, and which one you pick. In Deus Ex, you can choose how you level up through a tech tree that allows you to focus on combat effectiveness or stealth.

Emotional Weight
The game’s dramatic elements can make choices difficult, or even present situations as dilemmas when there isn’t a meaningful choice to make. Game narratives present choices all the time, with varying stakes. Games with units or parties can create dilemmas by putting characters in danger with tradeoffs.
Consider the Companion Cube from Portal, which was created opportunistically to create a moment of dilemma for the player.

Prisoner’s Dilemma
Zero Escape, Dead Last
Games can make use of social pressure and game theory to create difficult choices, especially when they involve other players. Dead Last is a tabletop game in which two players get to split an amount of loot. Each player can choose a small share or a large share, but if both players choose a large share then neither one gets anything, and the loot is instead distributed among the other players.
