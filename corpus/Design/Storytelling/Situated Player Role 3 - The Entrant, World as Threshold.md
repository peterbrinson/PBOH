The Entrant has arrived at a boundary. Unlike the Investigator, who researches the past, or the Traveler, who moves through space as an interpretive act, the Entrant confronts a blocked passage. Progress is possible — but only by satisfying conditions. 

These thresholds appear as gates, locked doors, guarded borders, or rituals. The player reads what is present in the space, tests what the system accepts or rejects, and gradually learns the rules governing passage. The room is a puzzle that compels the player to ask, **"How does this world work?"**

## The Shift to Control

The shift toward control is visible in what the mechanics ask the player to do. In *The Legend of Zelda* (1986), the player finds a key and uses it to change a locked door into an open passage. In *[[Game - Breath of the Wild|Breath of the Wild]]* (2017), the player discovers how the shrine's components behave, then reconfigures the room by rotating gears with Magnesis or freezing water with Cryonis.

## Types of Thresholds

##### Spatial Threshold

![[spr_entrant_02.webp]]

*[[Game - Portal|Portal]]* (2007) intensifies this role. Each chamber is a space the player wants to pass through, but the threshold is not a locked door — it is a non-Euclidean pathway.

##### Boss Threshold

![[spr_entrant_03.webp]]

In *[[Game - Elden Ring|Elden Ring]]*, the defining barriers are bosses. Progress halts until the player learns the logic of the fight: recognizing patterns, identifying vulnerabilities, and playing defensively.

##### Bureaucratic Threshold

![[spr_entrant_04.png]]

The Entrant does not always confront physical challenges. In *[[Game - Papers Please|Papers, Please]]* (2013), each interaction is a bureaucratic barrier. The player inspects documents, applies rules, and decides who may pass.


## The Entrant's Loop: L.O.P.

The Entrant operates through a repeatable three-step loop: **L.O.P.** — Legibility, Opportunity, and Performance.

The puzzle-platformer *[[Game - Inside|Inside]]* (2016) consistently manifests L.O.P.

![[spr_entrant_05.webp]]

**Legibility** ("See what matters.") — The player attends closely to the space, recognizing the barrier and identifying its interactive components. 

-----

![[spr_entrant_06.jpg]]

**Opportunity** ("Imagine the solution.") — By testing individual mechanics, the player discovers how the components could be configured or sequenced. Solutions exist first as speculations; opportunities arrive as epiphanies.

----------

![[spr_entrant_07.jpg]]

**Performance** ("Test the idea.") — The player acts upon the world, manipulating its components and testing whether the imagined solution works.

L.O.P. structures the Entrant's relationship to story by moving from discovery toward control. Legibility and Opportunity build understanding; Performance uses that understanding to act upon the world. The story lies in what the threshold asks the player to notice, learn, and do before passage becomes possible.

The Investigator reconstructs the events that produced the present situation. The Traveler experiences events as they unfold. The Entrant discovers what the present situation permits, then acts to change it.

In this way, the threshold itself becomes where the story finds its meaning — not simply something to pass, but something that defines the player’s relationship to the world.
