## What It Is
A first-person walk through vast, drained and half-flooded pool complexes — tiled corridors, filtration rooms, diving wells, and stairwells that keep going. Six chapters, ten to thirty minutes each. There is no combat, no inventory, no UI, no dialogue, and no music: only footsteps, water, and room tone. The developers describe it as being "like an art gallery where you look around and listen to the sounds," and are explicit that "there are no monsters chasing you or jumping suddenly towards you, but the game can feel oppressive."

It is the purest game-length expression of the [[World - Backrooms|Backrooms]] liminal-space aesthetic: a built environment emptied of people and purpose, still lit and still humming, whose scale and repetition do all the work that a monster would do elsewhere.

## Why It's Useful Here
**It separates dread from threat.** Almost every horror game teaches that tension comes from something that can hurt you. POOLS removes the thing entirely and keeps the tension, which makes it unusually good evidence when a student assumes atmosphere requires a pursuer.

**Its boundary is Closed.** The complex is enormous and keeps unfolding, but the player never reaches an outside — no exterior, no sky, no other people. The edge is felt as *more building*, not as a wall.

**It is a network labyrinth.** Rooms connect to rooms with no branching tree to solve and no center to reach; you keep moving and the space keeps continuing. Compare the unicursal and multicursal cases — here the illusion of freedom comes from sheer extent.
