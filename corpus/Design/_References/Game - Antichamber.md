## What It Is

A first-person puzzle and exploration game set in a seamless, non-Euclidean world. Corridors loop, rooms reconfigure, and ordinary assumptions about direction, distance, and cause and effect repeatedly fail.

## Expectation and Payoff

Antichamber uses familiar first-person movement and architectural forms to invite ordinary spatial predictions. A hallway appears to lead somewhere; turning around should return the player to the place they just left; a gap appears impossible to cross.

The world then contradicts those predictions, but each contradiction teaches a usable rule. Surprise becomes a learning rhythm: expectation, violation, reconsideration, and deliberate use. The payoff is not simply that the world is impossible, but that the player gradually learns how to think inside its impossibility.
