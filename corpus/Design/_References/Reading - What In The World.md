## Core Definition

> "The process of worldbuilding — the creation of imaginary worlds with coherent geographic, social, cultural, and other features." (p.32)

This is the foundational academic definition. Zaidi (2019) builds on it, adding that worlds "provide detailed contextual rule sets that develop a larger reality that extends beyond a single story." See [[Reading - Worldbuilding in Science Fiction, Foresight and Design (Zaidi)]].

---

## Four Categories of Speculative Fiction (p.6, citing Levin 2010)

Future-oriented science fiction tends to fall into one of four categories:

- **Cautionary tales** — emphasize consequences (generally negative) of some aspect of today's society
- **Thought experiments** ("what if" stories) — examine potential impacts of some current or anticipated event, technology, or trend
- **Literalized metaphors** — use a metaphor to study a particular aspect of our world (e.g., space aliens to address alienation from society), making it concrete
- **Explorations of new science and technology** — use new advances as the basis for a storyline

These categories are useful for thinking about which mode a world is operating in — or whether it's deliberately straddling modes.
