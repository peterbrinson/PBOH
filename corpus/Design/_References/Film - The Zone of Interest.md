## What It Is
The commandant of Auschwitz, Rudolf Höss, and his family live an ordinary domestic life in a house and garden that share a wall with the camp. The camera stays on their side: swimming, gardening, birthdays, household management. The camp is never shown. It arrives only as sound — gunshots, screams, the furnaces — and as smoke over the wall, and the family has learned not to notice.

## As a Bounded World
A **Closed** world (see [[Bounded Worlds]]). The garden wall is the edge, and the film never crosses it. The outside isn't hidden so much as refused — fully present through sound and ash, and kept out of view by choice. The horror is in the boundary itself: what a world must exclude to stay comfortable, and what it costs to keep the wall in place.
