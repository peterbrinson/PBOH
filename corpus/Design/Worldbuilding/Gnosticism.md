Gnosticism is a broad term for a group of early religious ideas and movements (mostly from the first few centuries CE) centered on one core claim:
**Salvation comes through special knowledge** — _gnosis_ (from the Greek for “knowledge”).

Gnostics tell a story in which the true God is not the creator of the world we see, but a distant, perfect source of being beyond it; from this source emanate divine aspects called **aeons**, one of which (often associated with wisdom, named Sophia) falls or fractures, leading to the creation of a lesser, ignorant creator—the Demiurge—who fashions the material universe as a flawed, incomplete copy of the divine. Humans, in this view, carry within them a fragment of that higher divinity, but live trapped in a world that obscures the truth of their origin. The central drama is not sin and forgiveness, but ignorance and awakening: a revealer figure (often identified with Christ) enters the world to remind humans of what they really are, offering hidden knowledge (gnosis) that allows them to see through the illusion of material reality and return, in awareness, to the divine source beyond it.

What happens in modern sci-fi—especially in writers like [[Reading - Philip K Dick|Philip K. Dick]]—is essentially a translation of Gnosticism into technological language. The old Gnostic story says: the world is a flawed reality created by a god. Science fiction rewrites this in contemporary terms: reality is a simulation. Philip K. Dick takes this even further—his characters constantly suspect that their world is fake, their memories implanted, their identities unstable (*Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*, *Ubik*, *VALIS*)—which mirrors the Gnostic sense that reality is a kind of veil. But instead of aeons and demiurges, Dick uses corporations, AI, media systems, and code—modern mechanisms that can plausibly generate a reality. So the shift is this: Gnosticism becomes sci-fi when mythological structures are replaced by technological ones, but the underlying intuition stays the same—that reality might be constructed and awakening means seeing beyond the apparent world.

## The Core Structure

A few terms worth knowing:

- **Demiurge** — the lesser creator god who built the material world. 
- **Pleroma** — the "fullness," the true divine realm that the demiurge's world is a pale copy of.
- **Gnosis** — direct experiential knowledge of your own true nature and the nature of the system. 
- **The spark** — the fragment of the pleroma that exists inside certain humans. It's what makes them capable of gnosis.
- **Archons** — agents of the demiurge that maintain the system and prevent awakening. (A sci-fi interpretation: the agents in The Matrix, the corporations in PKD, the media apparatus).

The structure is: nested realities, a flawed builder, and a hidden truth carried inside the people the builder made.

## Why This Is a Worldbuilding Problem

Gnosticism recognizes a worldbuilding act - someone built the world. 

## Examples

- [[Film - The Matrix|The Matrix]] — a simulated reality maintained by machines; a small group of humans discover the truth and must decide whether to fight the system or re-enter it.
- *Total Recall* (Verhoeven, 1990) — a man pays a company to implant the memory of a spy adventure on Mars; the film never resolves whether what follows is real or the implant running.
- *The Truman Show* (Weir, 1998) — a man has lived his entire life on a television set without knowing it; the show's creator watches from above and believes he's given Truman a good life.
- [[Game - Inscryption|Inscryption]] — a card game that reveals, layer by layer, that the reality containing it is itself inside another layer, which is inside another.
