A primary design challenge for video game designers is teaching the player how to play.  This is  teaching the rules of the world.  

Two approaches:

- **Explicit tutorial** — the world pauses to explain itself. Can feel artificial, but works when the rules are genuinely strange. *Soul* (2020) does this in film: when Joe arrives in the Great Before, the Jerrys explain how souls work before the story can continue.  (Necessary at Pixar's scale; likely a bad model for student projects).
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dmgsI-RSoyA

- **Teaching through play** — the early levels function as the tutorial. The world's rules emerge through low-stakes encounters before the player faces real consequences. [[Game - Portal|Portal]] is a clear example: each chamber introduces one mechanic, lets you learn, then hands you the next one. You discover the rules through play. [[Game - Baba Is You|Baba Is You]] takes this further: the rules are literally objects in the world..

This applies to the vast majority of video games. Almost every game has an early section — whether labeled a tutorial or not — where the world teaches you what it is before it asks anything hard of you.
