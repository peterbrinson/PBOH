Exposition becomes awkward when the world stops moving so it can explain itself — the cutscene, the text scroll, the block of [[Lore as a Trap|lore]]. The opposite instinct is embracing [[catalysts of speculation]]: a designed gap that encourages the player to imagine. 

## Graceful, Diegetic Options

Ways to deliver exposition that keep the player inside the world:

- **Environmental storytelling** — let the scene carry the history: a barricaded door, a skeleton still clutching its key, a room arranged by whoever lived in it ([[Game - Gone Home|Gone Home]]).
- **Object and item descriptions** — lore tucked into the things the player picks up and reads at their own pace ([[Game - Elden Ring|Elden Ring]]).
- **Diegetic documents and signage** — letters, notes, posters, and screens read in-world ([[Game - Return of the Obra Dinn|Obra Dinn]], [[Game - Papers Please|Papers, Please]]).
- **In-game codex** — an optional, player-paced archive of lore reached through a menu, graceful because it's opt-in— an entry that implies more than it answers (*Dark Souls* item descriptions) is a [[Catalysts of Speculation|catalyst of speculation]], rather than a [[Lore as a Trap|lore-dump]].
- **Audio logs and recordings** — voices left behind ([[Game - BioShock|BioShock]]).
