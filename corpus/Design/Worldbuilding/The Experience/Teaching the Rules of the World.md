A primary design challenge for video game designers is teaching the player how to play.  This is teaching the rules of the world.  

Two approaches:

- **Explicit tutorial** — the world pauses to explain itself.  This can feel artificial, but might be needed when the rules are intricate. *Soul* (2020) does this in film: when Joe arrives in the Great Before, the Jerrys explain how souls work before the story can continue.  (Workable at Pixar's scale; likely a bad model for student projects).
  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dmgsI-RSoyA

- **Teaching through play** — the early levels function as the tutorial. The world's rules emerge through low-stakes encounters before the player faces real consequences. [[Game - Portal|Portal]] is a clear example: early chambers each introduce one mechanic, invites you to learn through play, before you venture to the next one.  [[Game - Baba Is You|Baba Is You]] takes this further: the rules are literally objects in the world.

This aethetic aspiration comports with all genres.  Any early section — whether labeled a tutorial or not — teaches you the world's core systems before it moves on with complexity.
