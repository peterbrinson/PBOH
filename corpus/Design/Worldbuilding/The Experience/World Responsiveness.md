A world's responsiveness is what makes a space feel inhabited and exciting — it has precence, behavior, and consequence.

Let us craft our world's responsiveness with a pragmatic thread: a set of [[Tutorials - Unreal|Unreal Tutorials]] for beginners.  
To say a world is responsive is to say it reacts to the player's presence. A pressure plate opens a door. A light turns on when you enter a room. A sound plays when you step on the wrong tile. These are simple, but they establish: **the world knows you're there**.
More complex forms:
- Events triggered by what the player has done (not just where they are)
- World state that persists and changes over time
- Inhabitants who respond to the player's cumulative decisions, not just their current position

## The Haunted House as Model
The [[UE Tutorial 104 - Haunted House Triggers and Events|haunted house]] is a good framing because it makes explicit: the world is performing for the player. Lights, doors, NPCs, and sounds are the world's expressive vocabulary.
