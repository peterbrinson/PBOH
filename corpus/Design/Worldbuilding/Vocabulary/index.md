Mark Wolf's analytic terms — the language for describing a world: how it holds together, how far it departs from the real, what it's made of, and how it reaches across media.

**Three core properties.** A world earns its **plausibility** — the belief it asks of us — through three qualities working together:
- [[Mark Wolf's Taxonomy of Invention|Invention]] — how far the world departs from the real (the four kinds; folder: + Cultural, Nature)
- [[Completeness]] — a matter of degree; ancillary detail makes a world feel inhabited, and the gaps it leaves invite [[Catalysts of Speculation|speculation]]
- [[Consistency and Internal Logic]] — a world need not be realistic, but it must not contradict itself

**Structure & reach.** What the world is made of and how it travels:
- [[World Structure - Wolf's Eight Elements]] — three foundations + five content elements
- [[Narrative Structure in Worlds]] — how stories sit on top of a world
- [[Transmedia]] — one world across many works
