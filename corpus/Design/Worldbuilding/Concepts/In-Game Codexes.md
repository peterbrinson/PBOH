*Part of the [[Small Worlds]] framework.*

An in-game codex is an optional database of world lore accessible through a menu — history, species, factions, technology — delivered as text entries outside the flow of play.

Classic examples: the species and political entries in *Mass Effect*, item descriptions in *Dark Souls*, datapads in Star Wars games.

## The tension

Codexes sit right at the edge of [[Lore as a Trap|lore-dumping]]. They're information delivered in a block rather than discovered through experience. The counterargument is that they're **opt-in** — unlike a cutscene, the player chooses to read them.

The real question is how they're written:

- A codex entry that **answers every question** is [[Lore as a Trap|lore-dumping]] — the world explains itself completely and the imagination has nothing to do.
- A codex entry that **raises more questions than it answers** is closer to a [[Catalysts of Speculation|catalyst of speculation]] — the entry implies a vastness the game never shows.

*Dark Souls* item descriptions are the clearest example of the second type: a few sentences of oblique, fragmentary lore that imply entire histories without narrating them.
