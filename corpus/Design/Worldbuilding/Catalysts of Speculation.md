Wolf's term (p.60) — what the designer leaves **untold or unrealized** so that the audience imagines it. Not a gap from incompleteness, but a deliberate withholding that generates imaginative energy.

*Note*: this appears to be his own term; not found in wider use when googled.*

This is a kind of opposite of **[[Lore as a Trap|lore-dumping]]**, which is delivering large amounts of world backstory, history, or mythology to the audience in an undramatized block — through exposition, cutscenes, sometimes, text walls, or [[In-Game Codexes|in-game codexes]] — rather than letting it emerge through experience.  


## The PKD Connection

Philip K. Dick: *"Reality is that which, when you stop believing in it, doesn't go away."*  [[Reading - Philip K Dick]].

## Game Examples
- [[Game - Outer Wilds|Outer Wilds]] — the entire game is forensic reconstruction of a dead civilization. Nothing is told to you upfront. The world existed before you arrived and will exist after you're gone; you're just reading its traces.
- [[Game - Return of the Obra Dinn|Return of the Obra Dinn]] — you reconstruct what happened to a ship's crew entirely from frozen moments. The world is implied through evidence, never explained. The gaps *are* the game.
