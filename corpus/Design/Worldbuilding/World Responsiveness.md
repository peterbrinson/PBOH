A world's responsiveness is what makes a space feel *inhabited* — it has memory, behavior, and consequence.

*The following describes world responsiveness with a pragmatic thread*: our set of [[Tutorials - Unreal|Unreal Tutorials]].  
To say a world is responsive is to say it reacts to the player's presence. A pressure plate opens a door. A light turns on when you enter a room. A sound plays when you step on the wrong tile. These are simple, but they establish the most important thing: **the world knows you're there**.
More complex forms:
- Events triggered by what the player has done (not just where they are)
- World state that persists and changes over time
- Inhabitants who respond to the player's history, not just their current position

## The Haunted House as Model
The [[UE Tutorial 4 - Haunted House Triggers and Events|haunted house]] is a good framing because it makes explicit what's implicit in all responsive worlds: the world is *performing* for the player. Lights, doors, NPCs, and sounds are the world's expressive vocabulary.
