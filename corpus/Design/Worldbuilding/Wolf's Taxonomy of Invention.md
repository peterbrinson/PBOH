**Invention** = the degree to which default assumptions based on the primary (real) world have been changed (Wolf p.34). For example, a green sun is invention. In science fiction, we might want to know the rule behind that invention.  In fantasy, we do not expect to known.  

## Four Types (Wolf p.35)

1. **Nominal** — new names for real things. Credits instead of dollars. Realm instead of kingdom. The thing itself doesn't change; just the label.

2. **Cultural** — everything made by people: customs, technology, institutions, ideologies, objects, countries. This is the big one. Most of what we call "worldbuilding" in practice is cultural invention.

3. **Natural** — new landmasses, plants, animals, creatures. Hobbits, sandworms, unicorns. 

4. **Ontological** — parameters of the world's existence: materiality, laws of physics, space, time. Light speed, time travel, gravity. The deepest level of invention.

## Notes
- Common reused inventions across many projects: elves, clones, laser guns, antigravity (Wolf p.37). Reuse across examples establishes genre expectations that can then be subverted.
- Invention is the *step* from the real world to the fiction world.
